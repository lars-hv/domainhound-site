    How we counted

    On November 27, 2026, we counted one listed site per startup across 13 YC batches, from Winter 2023 through Fall 2026. Of 1,591 eligible companies, 10 had no usable site. A startup can change its domain after launch.

    Coverage, sources and terms

    We included public YC startups of any status with a listed location in a Bay Area town or city, or at Stanford. Each counted once. We left out fully remote companies and future batches.

    We took endings from the Public Suffix List so endings like .co.uk stay together. We measured the name just before the ending. To match brands and prefixes, we kept letters and digits, ignored case and dropped spaces and punctuation. We kept legal suffixes such as Inc.

    For name shapes, WordNet supplies English words. We split compounds at spaces, hyphens or capital letters, or into two words of at least three letters each. A single string of letters absent from the dictionary counts as “Possibly invented”. Other spellings go under “Other”.

    The sites come from YC OSS, an unofficial feed of the YC directory. YC’s terms of use restrict scraping and commercial reuse. The feed grants no separate data license. Domain Hound is independent of YC.

    Funding sources and .com checks

    We started with the 150 largest teams listed by YC and added companies with verified Form D filings. We included Emergent after checking its published round history. YC does not list its team size. Comparable revenue figures were unavailable.

    We added distinct public rounds and amounts sold in SEC filings. Corgi’s figure combines its named early rounds with three later filings. Its reported cumulative total differs from that sum. The table uses the six individual amounts.

    For each listed domain we checked its label with .com. “Yes” means the company listed that .com or it redirected to the listed site. “Registered only” means we confirmed registration without establishing a link to the company. Registration does not identify the owner.
