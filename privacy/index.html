Privacy

Last updated 25 November 2026

Domain searches run on your own computer. We run no search server and receive none of your searches or results. We receive a domain name and your email when you submit a watch on this site. We use no analytics, telemetry or cookies.

Who sees the names you check?

Your computer contacts public sources directly. Each service can see your IP address. If a check sends a name, the service sees that name too. Each service’s own privacy policy applies.

  • Domains and prices. Domain Hound checks domain registries through RDAP or WHOIS. It also checks DNS records and reads price lists from Spaceship, Porkbun, Dynadot and Gandi.
  • Trademarks and companies. It checks the USPTO trademark search, Norway’s Brønnøysund registers and company records from SEC EDGAR.
  • Apps, projects and handles. It looks for names on the Apple App Store, Google Play, GitHub, npm, PyPI, X and YouTube.
  • Names on the web. It can check Google autocomplete, Google Trends and Wikidata. It may visit the domain itself and check Tranco and Common Crawl for signs of use. If you set up Brave Search or Serper, it can send your search there too.

Domain Hound may save results on your computer so it can reuse them the next time you check.

Your AI assistant

Claude, Codex or whichever assistant you use sees the results Domain Hound sends back. The assistant provider’s privacy policy applies to those results.

Live price checks

If you ask for a live price, Domain Hound opens the shop’s public search page in a hidden browser on your computer. The shop sees the domain you check. Domain Hound does this without signing in or buying anything.

Optional domain watch alerts

Enter your email on this site. Your assistant only needs the domain. We store your email, the domains you ask us to watch, when you confirm and your news preference. We record the time and wording of your news choice.

Watching starts after you confirm by email. Each watch can send up to four emails, including confirmation. There are no scheduled digests.

  1. Confirm your watch. We include the registry status and expiry date when available. Our estimate of whether the name might come free is a guess.
  2. Expired. If the registry reports expiry or redemption, we let you know. The owner may still renew.
  3. About five days left. This alert goes out if the registry marks the name for deletion. The timing is approximate.
  4. Free to register. We double-check availability before sending this alert. We never buy or reserve the domain.

Rare Domain Hound news is optional. We use your address for watch emails only unless you opt in to news. You can unsubscribe from news and keep your watches.

Every watch email includes a signed manage link. It opens your watches without a password. You can turn off news, stop one watch or erase your address, news preference and all watches from our live records. Each watch email also has an erase link.

Cloudflare stores your watch details. The registry and the watched site receive the domain name when we check it. Resend delivers the emails. We never sell your details or share them for other purposes.

When you visit this site

GitHub Pages hosts this website. GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security. The fonts and images load directly from this site.

Source code and questions

Domain Hound is open source under the MIT license. You can read the code on GitHub to see every request the tool makes.

If you have a privacy question, open a GitHub issue.
