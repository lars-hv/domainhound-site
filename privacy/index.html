Privacy

Search on this website

GitHub Pages hosts this site, including its fonts and images. When you search, your browser sends your idea, name or full name to our online tool. For name ideas, the tool may send your idea and choices to Cloudflare Workers AI. It can reuse subject words and suggested names for up to 24 hours, keyed by a one-way code derived from your idea and choices. The watch form sends your details to our alert service only when you submit it.

Search on your computer

The local MCP tool runs on your computer. It sends us nothing, including names and results. It uses no cookies, analytics or telemetry.

Search with an online assistant

ChatGPT, Claude on the web and other online-only apps send us the names you check. Cloudflare runs the online tool and its website search. You need no account, email or cookie. For a domain check, we save the result alongside a one-way code made from the domain name. We keep no other logs of the names.

The online tool sends the name or domain to the services it needs to answer your search. Each service’s privacy policy applies to the lookups it receives.

  • Domains. The tool checks public RDAP and WHOIS records from domain registries and IANA, and DNS records through Cloudflare and Google.
  • Prices. The tool uses saved list prices from public pricing pages at Spaceship, Porkbun, Namecheap, Dynadot, Cloudflare and Gandi. A shop receives the domain if you open its search link.
  • Apps and trademarks. The tool looks up names in Apple’s US App Store catalog, Google Play and the US Patent and Trademark Office’s public search.
  • Public profiles and projects. The tool looks at public pages, embeds and records from TikTok, Instagram, X, YouTube, Threads, Facebook, Twitch and GitHub. The tool can also check npm and Bluesky. For LinkedIn and Reddit, the tool checks the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine and Common Crawl for public pages.

Who sees the names you check?

If the hosted service cannot check a .dev or .app domain, your browser may ask Google Registry directly and send the verdict back to Domain Hound.

If the hosted service cannot check the App Store, your browser may send the name directly to Apple. Apple can see your IP address.

With the local tool, your computer contacts public sources. Each service can see your IP address and any name sent to it. Its own privacy policy applies.

  • Domains and prices. We check RDAP, WHOIS and DNS records and read prices from Spaceship, Porkbun, Dynadot and Gandi.
  • Trademarks and companies. It checks the USPTO trademark search, Norway’s Brønnøysund registers and company records from SEC EDGAR.
  • Apps, projects and handles. It looks for names on the Apple App Store, Google Play, GitHub, npm and Bluesky.
  • Names on the web. It can check Google autocomplete, Google Trends and Wikidata. It may visit the domain itself and check Tranco and Common Crawl for signs of use. If you set up Brave Search or Serper, it can send your search there too.

The tool may save results on your computer for your next search.

Your AI assistant

Your assistant, such as Claude or Codex, sees your results. Its privacy policy applies to them.

Live price checks

For a live price, the tool opens the shop’s search page in a hidden browser on your computer. The shop sees the domain. The tool checks the price without signing in or buying anything.

Domain watch alerts

  • We store your email, the domains you watch, your sign-up and confirmation dates, and your name if you give it.
  • Sign up here or ask your assistant to send us your details. Your watch starts when you click the link we email you.
  • We send up to four emails per domain. One to confirm, then alerts if the domain expires, is due for release, or becomes available.
  • We use your data to provide the watch you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • We delete unconfirmed sign-ups after 7 days. We remove watches when they end or when you erase them. A backup copy can remain for up to 30 days. Every watch email has a link to see and stop your watches and a one-click link to erase your address, watches and news subscription.
  • We do not track opens or clicks in our emails.

Domain Hound news (optional)

  • If you say yes, we email you news about Domain Hound and other developer tools from BrightBoard AS. At most twice a month.
  • You can tick the empty box on our site and then confirm your watch by email. You can also choose “Confirm watch + news” in your confirmation email. If your AI assistant asks about news, your answer only chooses which button we highlight. Your click in the email is what counts. Saying no does not affect your watches.
  • We send news with your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and the Norwegian Marketing Control Act section 15).
  • We keep a record of when you said yes, where you signed up (site or assistant) and the exact words you agreed to.
  • Every news email has our postal address and a one-click unsubscribe link. You can also write to us. We stop at once, at the latest within 10 days.
  • When you unsubscribe or erase your data, we keep an address hash on our do-not-send list. The hash replaces your email address and prevents further news emails. This is our legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). We keep proof of consent for 3 years after you withdraw it. After erasure, it holds only the hash, consent text version and timestamps.
  • If we ever want to send a wider kind of email, we will ask you again.
